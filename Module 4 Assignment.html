Module 4 Applied Analysis: Visualizing Uncertainty and Communicating It Ethically

An online retailer is testing two email subject lines to determine which one generates more purchases from customers who receive the email.

The experiment is still in progress, but it has reached 65% of the planned sample size. The current results are:

  • Subject Line A: 2,146 purchases
  • Subject Line B: 2,081 purchases

The data collected so far includes all customer segments, and there is no evidence that the remaining 35% of the audience will behave differently.

The executive team will be meeting this week and have asked for an update on the current results and the likely outcome of the experiment.

Below is a visualization developed by a junior analyst in your team; while accurate, it does facilitate decision-making. You know that you are expected to recommend whether to stop the test early or continue collecting data.

Your Task:

Create one executive-ready visualization that communicates both the likely outcome of the test and the uncertainty that remains. Then write a brief executive explanation and an ethical reflection.

Part 1. Simulation or projection

Using Excel or R, estimate the likely final outcome under the assumption that the remaining 35% behaves like the data already observed. You should calculate the current margin, the projected final margin, and a 95% simulation-based interval for the final margin.

Part 2. Visualization

Create one visualization suitable for senior leadership. Your visualization should make the uncertainty visible rather than hiding it. It should be clean, uncluttered, and easy to interpret.

Part 3. Executive explanation

Write a short explanation (150–250 words) intended for senior leadership. Your explanation should:

  • Clearly summarize the current results Describe the likely final outcome, including any uncertainty
  • Interpret what the results mean for decision-making
  • Provide a recommendation (e.g., stop the test or continue), and briefly justify it

Part 4. Ethical reflection

Write a short reflection of 100 to 150 words explaining how this result could be presented in a misleading way, and what is the risk of presenting the result too confidently.

Submission Requirements:

  1. A word document with your executive explanation, the visualization, and ethical reflection
  2. Your Excel file with calculations or simulation; or your syntax and output if your results were computed in R.
